# CCA3: Comparative UX Evaluation

## Project Overview

This project focuses on evaluating and improving the user experience of existing e-commerce websites through redesigned low-fidelity and high-fidelity Figma prototypes. The evaluation examines the original website interfaces and the proposed redesigns to identify opportunities to improve usability, navigation, visual consistency, accessibility, and overall user satisfaction.

The project covers two websites: **Khelmart** and **Cultstore**.

A mixed-methods evaluation approach is used, combining qualitative Think Aloud testing with a quantitative UX survey covering 18 parameters.

## Methodology

The evaluation follows a mixed-methods approach:

1. **Participant Recruitment:** Five participants outside the project group are selected to reduce bias arising from familiarity with the design process.
2. **Original Website Testing:** Participants are asked to complete a predefined core task on the original website while verbalizing their thoughts, expectations, difficulties, and frustrations.
3. **Prototype Testing:** Participants perform the same task using the corresponding redesigned high-fidelity Figma prototype.
4. **Speech-to-Text Processing:** Audio recordings of the sessions are transcribed for qualitative analysis.
5. **Quantitative Survey:** Participants evaluate both versions using a comparative UX questionnaire covering 18 parameters.
6. **Comparative Analysis:** Qualitative feedback and quantitative ratings are examined to identify improvements, remaining usability challenges, and potential areas for further refinement.

## Key Findings and Results

The comparative evaluation is intended to assess how effectively the redesigned interfaces address usability challenges identified in the original websites.

The redesigns focus on providing a clearer interface structure, improving visual consistency, supporting intuitive navigation, and making important information and actions easier to locate.

The expected areas of evaluation include:

- **Navigation and discoverability:** Whether users can locate relevant products, categories, and interface options with greater ease.
- **Visual design and consistency:** Whether the redesigned layouts provide a more organized and consistent browsing experience.
- **Clarity and usability:** Whether interface elements and actions are understandable to users.
- **User satisfaction:** Whether participants find the redesigned experience more convenient and comfortable.
- **Remaining usability issues:** Whether users continue to encounter confusion, unnecessary steps, or other difficulties.

The final comparison will consider both positive and critical feedback. While the redesigned interfaces aim to provide a more intuitive and organized experience, the extent of improvement must be established through actual participant observations and survey responses.

**Overall Results:** The prototypes provide an opportunity to address potential usability shortcomings in the original websites through improved interface organization and design consistency. The final evaluation will determine the extent to which these changes translate into a better user experience.

## Final Conclusion

This project applies qualitative and quantitative usability evaluation techniques to examine the effectiveness of the redesigned Khelmart and Cultstore interfaces.

The high-fidelity prototypes represent proposed improvements to the original user interfaces. Their effectiveness will be assessed by comparing participants' experiences, identifying recurring usability concerns, and examining ratings across the 18 UX parameters.

A balanced conclusion will consider both the improvements observed and the issues that remain unresolved. The findings will inform further design refinements and help determine whether the proposed interfaces better support users in completing their intended tasks.

The final assessment will be based on the collected Think Aloud transcripts and survey results. Given the limited sample of five participants, the results will be interpreted as preliminary usability evidence rather than definitive proof of improvement for the entire user population.

## Limitations

The evaluation involves five participants, which is a relatively small sample and may not represent the experiences of the broader target audience.

Individual preferences, prior familiarity with e-commerce websites, testing conditions, and differences between a live website and a Figma prototype may influence the results.

The prototype evaluation may also be limited to the interactions and screens implemented in Figma. Therefore, the findings should be interpreted within the scope of the evaluation, and further testing with a larger and more diverse group of users may be beneficial.
